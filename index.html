Myths and best practices

Why does "Password123!" fail even though it meets complexity rules?

Those rules (one uppercase, one lowercase, one digit, one symbol) were designed before fast graphics-card cracking. People satisfy them the simplest way: capitalize a word and add 123 and ! at the end. Cracking tools try exactly those patterns in milliseconds.

What is Shannon entropy, and why is it better than a checklist?

Entropy counts how many equally likely possibilities an attacker must consider, in bits: H = L × log2(R) for random choices. It rewards length and randomness directly, instead of ticking boxes that people satisfy in predictable ways.

Why did NIST stop recommending regular password changes?

Forced changes lead people to make small, predictable edits (Summer1, then Summer2). NIST guidance (SP 800-63B) advises changing a password only when there is evidence of compromise, and favors length and blocklists of known-bad passwords over composition rules.

How do passkeys and two-step login fit in?

A strong password protects you if a site is breached and guessed offline. Two-step login and passkeys add protection if a password is stolen or phished. Use both: a unique strong password in a manager, plus a second step for email, banking and anything important.
